# Research: ProxyJump signed NEW review to trust-write boundaries

- Query: What is still required to order a one-time user-approved child host candidate with vault persistence, revocation and eventual retry after the test-only signed-review milestone?
- Scope: internal source and existing project contracts; design analysis only.
- Date: 2026-11-23

## Findings

### A reviewed signed candidate is not yet a write operation

The test ticket retains public metadata and a private review; its signed-review getters deliberately do not expose the digests (`SshHostKeyReviewMailbox.java:20-41,80-106`). `requireSignedNewFixtureApproved` checks the exact owner and role, runs the bounded wait, and returns `void`; the underlying decision is retired in `awaitDecision` (`:163-185,217-292`). A UI approval is not a durable one-shot commit receipt and neither it nor native `provisional_new_ready` represents effect admission. The native milestone is only one guarded child, signature/KEX and outgoing drain; it samples state and still rejects ordinary completion (`ssh_native_client.c:4385-4437`).

The current managed NEW path is deliberately incompatible with a non-authorizing pending ticket. `SshVaultAdapter.trustRequest/updateTrust` requires a `SshHostKeyAuthority.Proof`, exact snapshot, candidate and role (`SshVaultAdapter.java:192-262`; `VaultOperation.java:127-138,156-198`). Ordinary NEW `Proof.permitsCredentials()` is true (`SshHostKeyAuthority.java:165-201`); `requireVerified` constructs that proof after its ordinary review (`:270-283`). Reusing this object for an uncommitted child would mix review with credential authority. The role-less trust methods are documented compatibility seams, not signed-child authentication (`SshVaultAdapter.java:174-195`). Production `prepareJumpCallback` currently refuses NEW/CHANGED, including `StrictHostKeyChecking yes` (`SshPasswordConnector.java:1089-1103`); this remains the correct gate until a separate contract is implemented and tested.

### A generation sample does not reserve the candidate

The pending review stores a particular candidate identity, source, KEX cycle and private digests, then rechecks them on the owner (`SshNativeCallbackAdapter.java:165-205,208-277,1110-1159`). `requireProvisionalFixtureCandidate` explicitly promises only a point-in-time identity check under the provider monitor, not reservation or write permission (`SshManagedKnownHostsProvider.java:602-615`). By contrast, `proposalForExact` can consume and close that candidate while holding the provider monitor (`:770-813`; candidate one-shot behavior at `:485-500`); that method itself does not advance the jump-effect generation. The synthetic fixture explicitly consumes or substitutes a candidate and checks the review rejects it (`SshFreshVaultRealJniTest.java:260-286,1032-1037`); replacement there uses reflection and is test-only, so it is not evidence of a public product exploit.

Therefore a future design must establish a single-owner, one-shot transfer or reservation for the exact candidate in addition to checking generation. A sequential candidate check, followed by an unrelated native generation permit, cannot on its own show that the same candidate still belongs to the eventual writer. Any such transfer must coordinate with cancellation and abort without holding a short native permit while acquiring the provider/receipt locks: provider cleanup sets `closed`, takes an invalidation, and later wipes the independent candidate/proposal and receipt (`SshManagedKnownHostsProvider.java:976-1033`). The precise safe transfer linearization and its interaction with every possible candidate mutator are not implemented or proven here.

### Revocation, time and durability require distinct ordering points

The native effect word holds one process-wide generation, invalidator count and in-flight count (`ssh_role_bootstrap.c:26-35`). Snapshot and CAS acquire reject an active invalidator or a mismatched generation, and invalidation increments the generation then waits for acquired effects to drain (`:43-67,87-120`). Owner `Cancellation.cancel()` holds its monitor during this drain (`SshSessionOwner.java:26-47`); role close and vault session lock likewise begin invalidation while holding their own monitors (`SshRoleContext.java:191-195`; `VaultSession.java:71-80`). If an admitted effect takes one of those monitors or enters provider/session/vault while an invalidator waits, the two can deadlock. The short permit must cover only a demonstrably bounded operation with a proven lock order; filesystem writes, JNI callbacks and owner/vault/session/provider monitor acquisition cannot simply be inserted inside it.

Generation does not encode elapsed time. `ctos_ssh_effect_acquire` compares only generation and counts, while the native wire guard separately checks `CLOCK_BOOTTIME` against the original deadline before and immediately after acquire (`ssh_role_bootstrap.c:43-67`; `ssh_native_client.c:420-453`). A future write/seal admission needs its own precise deadline rule at the actual admission boundary. A prior Java role check and a matching generation do not by themselves show that the original hop deadline had not expired before admission. Even an admitted bounded operation may physically finish later if its owner is descheduled; its contract must name what admission, expiry and cancellation ordering actually guarantee.

`SshSessionOwner.Cancellation.confirmTrust` orders a per-scope one-shot confirmation with its cancellation state (`SshSessionOwner.java:48-72`). The direct-path contract deliberately preserves possible-write uncertainty until actual native acknowledgement and denies credentials while pending (`.trellis/spec/backend/ssh-trust-commit-ack.md:1-24`). The current per-hop ledger is set `PENDING` by `newPersisted()` only after `adapter.updateTrust` returns (`SshManagedKnownHostsProvider.java:831-852`; `SshPasswordConnector.java:483-517`). Its existing confirmation is therefore not a pre-write reservation and is not, by itself, a native ancestor-wide trust-write admission. `mayWriteTrust && creating` defers selected screen-lock/service results during the current synchronous factory (`SshSessionOwner.java:127-151,220-252`); moving the transaction to a new phase must preserve an equivalent in-flight result contract for the entire new phase rather than assuming that boolean always covers it.

The reusable storage path still provides material protections: `captureSshTrust` checks the exact operation/proposal and captures the current content revision, session key, ciphertext and independent candidate copy (`AppSecrets.java:2027-2083`); it rechecks the unchanged ciphertext and selected known-hosts object/base digest (`:2128-2135,2175-2181,2232-2239`). `VaultOperation` is consumed once (`AppSecrets.java:2040-2062`), so a failed attempt cannot silently reuse that operation. These checks do not establish that a newly created post-teardown operation captured the *original* reviewed source: the new contract must independently retain and compare the frozen original vault identity, binding, epoch/revision, path, object identity, base/replacement digests, endpoint and exact signed session evidence.

`VaultStore.commit` writes and fsyncs a temporary AtomicFile before `guard.beforeCommit`, then invokes `finishWrite` and reads back (`VaultStore.java:46-81`). Thus an admission at the final guard can prevent an authoritative trust replacement when revocation wins, but does not mean literally no temporary filesystem work has happened. `AppSecrets.commitEncoded` increments `contentRevision` and initiates native invalidation inside `beforeCommit`; `updateTrust` then advances the vault epoch and treats later failure after its `persisted` flag as uncertain (`AppSecrets.java:2184-2229,2257-2262,3384-3399`; `VaultSession.java:98-106`). A revision increment alone is not proof that `finishWrite` persisted, and a generation change alone cannot identify the writer's own invalidation. A write that may have committed without confirmed acknowledgement must remain `VAULT_COMMIT_UNCERTAIN`; a conclusively rejected pre-commit effect should not be mislabeled as a confirmed write. Do not hold a native effect permit through `sync`, `finishWrite`, readback or a vault lock.

### Live-write and retire-then-write are separate unproven designs

Keeping the old chain alive while committing can reuse the exact live source checks and the encrypted candidate/CAS mechanics, but no current signed-only NEW operation can use them. The writer's own commit invalidates the effect generation and advances epoch/revision. The child snapshot remains bound to the old epoch/revision (`SshManagedKnownHostsProvider.java:237-279`) and the product connector rejects changed revision when `jumpHops != null` (`SshPasswordConnector.java:1943-1953`). A correct first implementation could stop the temporary chain after confirmed persistence and require a fresh request; it cannot continue credentials on the stale source by accepting `revision + 1` or recapturing the latest generation without proving intervening invalidation provenance.

Alternatively, sealing the exact signed review and candidate, completing planned teardown, and then writing could avoid a live SSH chain during storage, but it requires a new detached capability and is not implemented. Existing receipt liveness calls the original role (`SshManagedKnownHostsProvider.java:135-142`), child owner finish closes its source and disables the effect fence (`SshNativeSession.java:1062-1066,1163-1177`), and provider close wipes candidate/proposal (`SshManagedKnownHostsProvider.java:976-1033`). `SshPasswordConnector.close()` calls `cancel()` before reverse chain cleanup (`SshPasswordConnector.java:552-577,1225-1249,2030-2034`), so it cannot serve unchanged as a non-cancelling planned retirement for a detached write.

After the old view closes, `VaultSession.attachedRevocation` can still return a non-null inactive object, while a new active view can attach when the old one is inactive (`VaultSession.java:83-105`; `SshPublicFileLease.java:139-154`). The current role-bound continuation requires the exact active role/view (`AppSecrets.java:2010-2024,2063-2071`). The role-less `unlockIfEpoch` path invokes `lock()`, which can revoke a currently attached view (`VaultSession.java:33-38,71-80`). A detached writer therefore needs a proved, cancellation-aware exclusive reservation or another exact view/epoch rule and explicit uncertainty semantics for attachment races. Merely clearing a source pointer or using the old role-less adapter does not solve ownership.

### Retry is not the same as completing the trust write

On a failed connection, `SshSessionOwner` may complete its future before `closeUnattached` finishes retryable cleanup (`SshSessionOwner.java:186-206,335-350`). `SshPasswordConnectBridge` clears its active attempt when that future completes (`SshPasswordConnectBridge.java:473-506`), but `SshSessionService.attach` still returns `BUSY` until its static owner is actually cleared, potentially only in matching `onDestroy` (`SshSessionService.java:68-75,275-297`). Any retry-ready result must be bound to the exact cleaned and service-released owner, not merely the earlier future or a logical `released` callback.

The config selection binding incorporates snapshot binding, epoch, revision and alias (`SshConnectionMaterial.java:524-527`); a successful trust write therefore invalidates the old selection binding. A fresh attempt must re-read and revalidate alias, actual endpoint, user and policies, with fresh per-hop confirmations. The Flutter request resets hop counters for a new request (`password_connect_page.dart:1445-1460`); an internal same-request restart can regress hop order and is rejected (`:1758-1769,1881-1883`). Explicit new requests are a candidate simplification, not an implemented product behavior.

### Suggested next tests and ordering questions

1. Define exactly which actor owns the candidate before review, after approval, after transfer, during any planned retirement and after a failed/uncertain write. At each transition inject consume, abort, source close, role/view revocation, owner stop and deadline crossing; verify the wrong candidate or owner cannot consume a valid receipt. Candidate identity mutation must be tested independently of native generation changes.
2. At exact seal/admission boundaries race cancellation, screen lock/background, ancestor EOF/close, role/view revocation, unrelated vault write and original hop expiry. Confirm whether revocation began before or after the defined linearization point, whether any operation was already admitted, and whether the result is certainly unwritten or uncertain. Test with barriers rather than relying only on post-checks or time sleeps.
3. Test encrypted in-memory Store and actual AtomicFile separately: ciphertext preparation, before-commit guard, confirmed rollback, finish/readback uncertainty, self-induced generation/revision/epoch changes and acknowledgement. Prove no provider/session/vault monitor is acquired by a short permit holder, and no native permit spans filesystem I/O.
4. For planned teardown, independently inject failure at child, relay, callback, provider, source, view and role cleanup; retain exact owners and do not start a detached write before all required cleanup is confirmed. Race a new view attach and distinguish it from planned retirement. Preserve primary failure and sensitive-buffer cleanup without claiming unknown invalidations succeeded.
5. Delay owner cleanup and matching service `onDestroy`, attempt immediate retry, and reorder form/terminal events; require a fresh config binding and independent per-hop password confirmations. Verify known-host, StrictHostKeyChecking `yes`, direct and already-supported saved-password jump regressions separately from synthetic NEW and actual Android/device evidence.

## Caveats / Not Found

- This analysis did not implement a trust admission, transfer, detached receipt, planned retirement, write, confirmation or reconnect. No new tests were run by this research.
- The suggested alternatives are not proven implementations. A generation-bound publication must account for candidate mutation, deadline expiry and every relevant invalidator, as well as a lock order that cannot deadlock.
- The test-only signed review and native bounded polling are independently implemented; they are currently under active integration by another agent. Neither this note nor their connection authorizes product trust, authentication, credentials or a persisted write.
- Historical design and test evidence is scoped to the cited implementation snapshot. In particular the JNI candidate replacement test uses a test-only reflective mutation; it does not show that an arbitrary app caller can do so.
